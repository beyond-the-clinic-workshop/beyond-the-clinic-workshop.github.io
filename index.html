Beyond the Clinic

AI for Patient-Driven Health

Patients are increasingly managing their health outside of the clinic, where AI now interacts with people directly. We ask what health AI must get right when people collect, interpret, and act on their own data.

About

Most health happens outside the clinic

Health AI has largely been built around clinical workflows, which supply measurement protocols, curated labels, and a professional who reviews outputs before anyone acts on them. Yet most health is managed outside the clinic, where people increasingly collect their own data, read their own records, and interpret results alone or with AI.

We call this patient-driven health: health decisions initiated and steered by the individual, whether or not a clinician is involved. Foundation models are already trained on wearable data from millions of individuals, and one chatbot fields health questions from over 230 million people a week.

This workshop asks what AI must get right in this setting, and how the data people collect can in turn advance science where clinical research has lagged. Through a debate, roundtables, and panels spanning AI, clinical, and patient perspectives, we aim to lay methodological groundwork for health AI that people can safely act on.

Three levels of patient-driven health

1

Detect

Spotting elevated risk or undiagnosed disease, such as glucose spikes into prediabetic ranges in people whom standard tests classify as normal.

2

Manage

Managing known risks and chronic conditions day to day, from diabetes to tracking cycles to understand symptoms.

3

Improve

Improving health and wellness without a known risk or condition, from sleep and fitness to athletic performance.

Call for papers

Open problems

We invite submissions on methods, data, evaluation, and applications for AI that people use to understand and act on their own health. Topics include, but are not limited to:

01

Translating between population evidence and individual experience

How should models weigh population evidence against a person’s own data, and how can personal data build new population evidence?

  • Adapting population knowledge to individuals
  • The reference class problem when individuals were not represented in the study population
  • Personalization from small samples and the risk of overfitting to the individual
  • N-of-1 designs and individual causal inference
  • Pooling patient-generated data to build new population evidence
  • Personal baselines that change over time: online and test-time adaptation
02

Learning from personal health data

How can models learn reliably from data that are incomplete, noisy, and self-selected?

  • Foundation models and self-supervised learning for wearable and multimodal data
  • Reasoning over heterogeneous personal records, from medical records to wearable and self-reported data
  • Learning without curated labels and evaluation without per-person ground truth
  • Informative missingness and measurement without protocol
  • Robustness to differences in devices, sensors, and firmware
  • Data self-selection and performance across subgroups
03

Adaptive interventions and sequential decision-making

How can systems learn to act for an individual safely and effectively, when every intervention changes the behavior and data they learn from?

  • Methods for adaptive and closed-loop interventions
  • Just-in-time adaptive interventions and micro-randomized trials
  • Feedback loops between interventions, behavior, and data
  • Off-policy and counterfactual evaluation of adaptive systems
  • Online personalization and test-time adaptation
  • Optimization with long-term, delayed, and proxy outcomes
  • Safe exploration and constrained decision-making
04

Human–AI interaction and safety

How can AI help people understand and act on their health information safely, without a professional in the loop?

  • Evaluating LLMs’ ability to interpret and synthesize patient-provided records, lab results, and clinical notes
  • Uncertainty quantification and out-of-distribution detection for knowing when not to answer
  • Active information seeking in response to patient-driven prompting
  • Measuring and mitigating over-reliance on models and model sycophancy
  • Identifying and mitigating harmful outputs and misuse
  • Designing outputs robust to misinterpretation by non-experts
  • Patient privacy and data protection
  • Evaluating outcomes rather than outputs: human–AI team performance, user studies and trials, person-defined endpoints

We especially welcome submissions from application areas such as women’s health, mental health, and sports performance.

Submission tracks

Up to 2 pages

Tiny papers

Early findings and late-breaking developments that would benefit most from discussion: a simple idea implemented and evaluated for the first time, a small self-contained theoretical result, or a new perspective on an existing paper.

The five top-rated tiny papers give three-minute lightning talks before the first poster session.

Up to 4 pages

Long papers

More developed, unpublished work, such as new methods, datasets, benchmarks, or empirical studies.

Five are selected for five-minute spotlight talks before the second poster session.

Program

An interactive day

Beyond the invited talks, the day centers on roundtables, panels, and a debate, with dedicated audience Q&A in every session.

Roundtables

Speakers, panelists, organizers, and attendees talk freely in small groups under the Chatham House Rule. An organizer at each table collects the questions that drew the most interest, and the moderators put the strongest ones to the stage, so a question you would hesitate to ask in front of the full room can still be heard.

Live-vote debate

Attendees vote for or against the motion from their phones before the opening statements and can switch sides at any point. The running count stays on screen, and the side that gains the most ground by the closing vote wins.

Expert panels

Panels bring ML developers, clinicians, and patient advocates into the same conversation, each with dedicated audience Q&A.

Talks into posters

Contributed lightning and spotlight talks lead straight into the two poster sessions, so discussion continues at the posters.
